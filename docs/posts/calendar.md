Building software isn’t a straight line—especially when new ideas and edge cases
show up mid-flight. This hasn't changed in the era of AI generated applications.

I used Uzzo Workflow to take a simple cross-platform calculator app from an
initial idea to working software. Instead of treating scoping, user stories, and
implementation as locked-in waterfall stages, the workflow keeps the product owner
and AI aligned on a shared knowledge base while letting decisions evolve naturally.

A few highlights from how that iteration played out:
* Clarifying before committing: Starting with a basic calculator idea led to
  targeted questions—from desktop vs. browser and keyboard/keypad input to
  arithmetic rules.
* Adjusting scope without losing context: Adding a 20-entry session history,
  merging domains, adding platform setup stories (Windows, macOS, Linux), and
  locking in JavaFX 23 all updated the scope and story index cleanly.
* Course-correcting during implementation: After reviewing the history behavior,
  I changed direction to a hidden history navigated with Up/Down arrow
  keys—updating the story and moving forward without rewriting everything from
  scratch.

It is noticeable how a living knowledge base keeps requirements, user stories,
story statuses, and tests connected as your understanding of the product sharpens.
